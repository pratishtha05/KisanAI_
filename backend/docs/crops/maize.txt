MAIZE (Zea mays) - CROP OVERVIEW

Growing Season and Climate:
Maize is a versatile crop grown in kharif (June-July sowing), rabi (October-November sowing) and spring seasons in India. It requires temperatures of 21-27°C for optimal growth and is more drought-tolerant than rice but still water-sensitive during flowering (tasseling and silking).

Soil Requirements:
Well-drained loamy soils with good organic matter content are ideal. pH range of 5.5-7.5 is suitable. Maize does not tolerate waterlogging; even short periods of standing water during early growth can cause significant yield loss.

Seed Rate and Sowing:
Seed rate is typically 20-25 kg/hectare for normal density hybrids, sown at a spacing of 60x20 cm. Sowing depth should be 3-5 cm. Line sowing with a seed drill gives better plant stand than broadcasting.

Growth Stages:
1. Germination and emergence (0-10 days)
2. Vegetative stage - V-stages, knee-high to waist-high (10-40 days)
3. Tasseling and silking (reproductive stage, 40-65 days) - most critical for water stress
4. Grain filling / milk-dough stage (65-90 days)
5. Physiological maturity - black layer formation (90-110 days depending on hybrid)

Common Varieties/Hybrids:
Pioneer P3396, DKC-9108, PMH-1, PMH-13 (Punjab), and various single-cross hybrids adapted to region-specific pest and disease pressure.

Critical Water-Sensitive Period:
The two weeks around tasseling and silking (flowering) are the most water-critical stage; moisture stress at this time can cause 20-50% yield loss due to poor pollination and kernel set (barrenness).

Yield Expectations:
Hybrid maize under good management can yield 60-90 quintals/hectare; yields drop sharply if flowering coincides with drought stress or excessive heat above 35°C.

Harvesting:
Harvest when husks turn brown/dry and kernel moisture is around 20-25% for mechanical harvesting, or when a black layer forms at the kernel base indicating physiological maturity.
