RICE (Oryza sativa) - CROP OVERVIEW

Growing Season and Climate:
Rice is the principal kharif (monsoon) crop in India, transplanted or direct-seeded between June and July and harvested between October and November. It requires a warm, humid climate with temperatures of 20-35°C and abundant water availability, since it is typically grown under standing water (puddled transplanted rice) though direct-seeded and aerobic rice systems are increasingly used to save water.

Soil Requirements:
Clayey or clay-loam soils with good water-retention capacity are ideal for transplanted rice, since these soils hold standing water effectively. pH range of 5.5-6.5 is optimal. For direct-seeded rice, well-drained loamy soils are preferred to avoid waterlogging at the seedling stage.

Nursery and Transplanting:
For transplanted rice, seedlings are raised in a nursery for 25-35 days before transplanting into puddled main fields at a spacing of approximately 20x15 cm, giving roughly 33 hills per square metre. 2-3 seedlings per hill is standard practice.

Growth Stages:
1. Seedling stage (nursery, 0-30 days)
2. Vegetative/tillering stage (transplanting to panicle initiation, 30-60 days after transplanting)
3. Panicle initiation and booting (60-80 days)
4. Flowering (80-95 days)
5. Grain filling / milk-dough stage (95-115 days)
6. Maturity and harvest (115-140 days depending on variety duration)

Common Varieties:
PR-126, PR-121, Pusa-44 (long duration, being phased out due to high water/stubble burning concerns), PR-114, Basmati varieties (Pusa Basmati-1509, Pusa Basmati-1121) for premium export markets.

Water Management:
Traditional transplanted rice requires continuous standing water of 5 cm during most of the vegetative stage, though Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) can reduce water use by 15-30% without significant yield loss when managed correctly.

Yield Expectations:
Yields of 60-70 quintals/hectare (paddy) are achievable for high-yielding non-basmati varieties under good irrigation; basmati varieties typically yield lower (35-45 quintals/hectare) but fetch a premium market price.

Harvesting:
Harvest when 80-85% of grains in the panicle have turned golden yellow and moisture content is around 20-22%. Delayed harvest increases shattering and quality loss.
