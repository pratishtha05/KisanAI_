MUSTARD (Brassica juncea) - CROP OVERVIEW

Growing Season and Climate:
Mustard is a rabi oilseed crop, sown between mid-October and mid-November and harvested between February and March. It requires a cool, dry climate during vegetative growth and a relatively warm, dry period during pod maturation. It is more drought-tolerant than wheat and is often grown as a rainfed or limited-irrigation crop.

Soil Requirements:
Well-drained loamy to sandy-loam soils are preferred. Mustard tolerates mild soil salinity and slightly alkaline conditions better than most rabi crops, making it a common choice in marginal or saline soils. pH range of 6.0-7.5 is optimal, though it tolerates up to pH 8.0.

Seed Rate and Sowing:
Seed rate is typically 4-5 kg/hectare for pure stand sowing. Row spacing is 30-45 cm with plant-to-plant spacing of 10-15 cm after thinning. Sowing depth should be shallow, around 2-3 cm, since mustard seeds are small.

Growth Stages:
1. Germination and seedling establishment (0-15 days)
2. Vegetative/rosette stage (15-35 days)
3. Stem elongation and branching (35-55 days)
4. Flowering (55-75 days) - critical stage for irrigation and pest watch (aphids)
5. Pod formation and seed development (75-100 days)
6. Maturity and harvest (100-140 days depending on variety)

Common Varieties:
Pusa Bold, RH-30, Varuna, Giriraj, Pusa Vijay - selected based on region, irrigation availability, and disease pressure (particularly white rust and Alternaria blight).

Water Management:
Mustard is often grown with just 1-2 irrigations. A pre-flowering irrigation (around 30-35 days after sowing) and a irrigation at pod-filling stage significantly boost yield; excess irrigation encourages vegetative growth and lodging.

Pest Pressure:
Mustard aphid (Lipaphis erysimi) is the single most damaging pest, typically appearing from January onward and capable of causing severe yield loss if uncontrolled during flowering and pod formation.

Yield Expectations:
Under good management with limited irrigation, yields of 18-22 quintals/hectare are achievable; purely rainfed mustard yields around 8-12 quintals/hectare.

Harvesting:
Harvest when pods turn yellowish-brown and seeds rattle inside when shaken. Delayed harvest leads to pod shattering and seed loss, particularly in windy conditions.
