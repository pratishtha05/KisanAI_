Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum) — Package of Practices

Crop Profile
- Season: Planted year-round in different regions; main planting windows are Autumn (Sept-Oct) and Spring (Feb-Mar)
- Suitable soil: Deep, well-drained loam to clay-loam, pH 6.5–7.5
- Duration: 10–18 months depending on planting season and variety (a long-duration crop compared to cereals)

Seed Selection
- Propagated from stem cuttings ("setts"), not true seed
- Recommended varieties (North India): Co-0238, CoJ-64, CoS-8436
- Sett rate: 35,000–40,000 three-budded setts per hectare (roughly 6–8 tonnes of cane used as seed material per hectare)
- Sett treatment: Soak setts in fungicide solution (e.g., Carbendazim 0.1%) for 15–20 minutes before planting to prevent sett rot and red rot disease.

Sowing / Planting
- Autumn planting window: mid-September to mid-October (gives highest yield potential, longer growing season)
- Spring planting window: mid-February to mid-March
- Row spacing: 90–120 cm between furrows
- Planting depth: setts laid horizontally in furrows 5–8 cm deep, covered with soil
- Method: Furrow planting; trench planting used in high-wind or lodging-prone areas for better anchorage.

Fertilizer Schedule
- Recommended dose (per hectare): N:P:K = 250:60:60 kg/ha (spread across the long growing season, not applied all at once)
- Basal dose: Full P and K, plus 1/3rd N at planting
- 1st top dressing: 1/3rd N at 6–8 weeks after planting (tillering stage)
- 2nd top dressing: remaining 1/3rd N at 12–14 weeks after planting (grand growth phase)
- Sugarcane responds strongly to organic manure — 10–15 tonnes/ha farmyard manure incorporated before planting improves soil structure over the long crop duration.

Irrigation Schedule
- Critical stages: germination (first 6–8 weeks), tillering, and the grand growth phase (peak water demand) are most sensitive to water stress.
- Irrigation interval: every 7–10 days during summer months, 15–20 days in cooler months, adjusted for rainfall.
- Total water requirement is high (1800–2500 mm over the crop cycle) — among the most water-intensive common crops, which matters for water-stressed regions.
- Avoid waterlogging, especially in the first 2 months — poor drainage at planting causes sett rot and poor germination.

Common Risks
- Red rot disease risk increases in waterlogged or poorly drained fields — avoid replanting the same field with sugarcane consecutively (crop rotation reduces buildup).
- Early shoot borer and top borer are the major pest concerns in the first 3–4 months — pheromone traps and timely detrashing (removing dry leaves) help control them.
- Lodging (crop falling over) risk increases with excess N and inadequate earthing-up (mounding soil around the base) during the grand growth phase.
