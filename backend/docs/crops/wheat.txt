WHEAT (Triticum aestivum) - CROP OVERVIEW

Growing Season and Climate:
Wheat is a rabi (winter) crop in India, sown between late October and late November and harvested between March and April, depending on region and variety. It requires a cool growing season with temperatures between 10-25°C and a relatively warm, dry period during grain-filling and maturity. Excessive heat during grain filling (above 30°C) reduces yield significantly by shortening the grain-filling window.

Soil Requirements:
Wheat performs best on well-drained loamy or clay-loam soils with good water-holding capacity. Ideal soil pH ranges from 6.0 to 7.5. Waterlogged or poorly drained fields lead to root diseases and poor tillering. Soil should be ploughed 2-3 times before sowing to achieve a fine tilth.

Seed Rate and Sowing:
Recommended seed rate is approximately 100 kg/hectare for normal sowing and up to 125 kg/hectare for late sowing (to compensate for reduced tillering). Row spacing is typically 20-22.5 cm. Sowing depth should be 5-6 cm; deeper sowing delays emergence and weakens seedlings.

Growth Stages:
1. Germination (0-10 days after sowing)
2. Crown Root Initiation - CRI (18-25 days) - critical stage for first irrigation
3. Tillering (25-45 days)
4. Jointing/Stem elongation (45-65 days)
5. Booting and heading (65-85 days)
6. Flowering/Anthesis (85-95 days)
7. Milk stage and dough stage (95-115 days)
8. Physiological maturity and harvest (115-140 days depending on variety)

Common Varieties (North India / Punjab region):
HD-2967, PBW-725, PBW-343, WH-1105, DBW-187, HD-3086. Variety selection should account for local disease pressure (especially rust) and sowing window (timely vs late sown).

Yield Expectations:
Under good management with irrigation, yields of 45-55 quintals/hectare are achievable for high-yielding varieties in Punjab/Haryana conditions. Late-sown or rainfed wheat yields considerably lower, often 25-35 quintals/hectare.

Harvesting:
Harvest when grains are hard and moisture content is around 20-25% for combine harvesting, or slightly higher for manual harvesting followed by sun-drying. Delayed harvest increases shattering losses and exposure to untimely rain.
