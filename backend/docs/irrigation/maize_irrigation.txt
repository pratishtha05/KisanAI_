MAIZE IRRIGATION SCHEDULE AND GUIDANCE

Number of Irrigations:
Maize typically requires 4-6 irrigations during its growth cycle under rainfed-supplemental or fully irrigated conditions, depending on rainfall distribution during the kharif season or irrigation availability during rabi/spring maize.

Critical Irrigation Stages:
1. Knee-high stage (25-30 days after sowing) - supports vegetative growth and root establishment.
2. Pre-tasseling (40-45 days after sowing).
3. Tasseling and silking (55-65 days after sowing) - THE most critical stage; water stress during this narrow window (7-10 days) can cause 20-50% yield loss due to poor pollen viability and incomplete silk emergence, resulting in poorly filled cobs or barrenness.
4. Grain filling / milk stage (70-85 days after sowing) - water stress here reduces kernel weight.
5. Dough stage (90-100 days after sowing, optional depending on soil moisture).

Rainfall Interaction:
Because the tasseling-silking window is so short and critical, even if recent rainfall has occurred, soil moisture should be actively monitored during this period rather than assuming rainfall alone is sufficient — light or scattered rainfall (under 15mm) is often not enough to meet peak crop water demand during flowering, which can exceed 6-7 mm/day in hot weather.

Water Quantity:
A single irrigation for maize should supply approximately 5-6 cm of water. Maize does not tolerate waterlogging at any stage; even 24-48 hours of standing water can cause significant root damage and yield loss, so field drainage is as important as irrigation scheduling.

Signs of Water Stress in Maize:
Leaf rolling during the hottest part of the day (which does not recover by evening), a grayish-green leaf color, and delayed silk emergence relative to tassel emergence (poor synchronization) are indicators that irrigation is needed immediately.

Drip Irrigation:
For high-value or water-scarce maize cultivation, drip irrigation can improve water use efficiency by 30-40% compared to flood irrigation and allows for more precise scheduling during the critical tasseling-silking window.
