RICE IRRIGATION SCHEDULE AND GUIDANCE

Traditional Continuous Flooding:
Transplanted rice is traditionally grown under continuous standing water of about 5 cm depth from transplanting until about 10 days before harvest. This method is water-intensive, typically requiring 1200-1500 mm of water over the season depending on soil type and climate.

Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD):
AWD is a water-saving irrigation technique where the field is allowed to dry until the water table drops to about 15 cm below the soil surface (measured using a perforated field water tube), at which point the field is re-flooded to 5 cm depth. This cycle is repeated through the vegetative stage. AWD can reduce total irrigation water use by 15-30% compared to continuous flooding, with minimal or no yield penalty when managed correctly, and also reduces methane emissions.

Critical Water-Sensitive Stages (should NOT be allowed to dry out even under AWD):
1. Active tillering (15-40 days after transplanting)
2. Panicle initiation (around 55-60 days after transplanting)
3. Flowering/anthesis (around 80-90 days after transplanting) - most sensitive stage; even brief water stress here can cause spikelet sterility and severe yield loss.

Stages Where Field Can Be Safely Dried:
Approximately 10 days before harvest, irrigation should be stopped entirely to allow the field to dry for easier harvesting and to improve grain quality and reduce lodging.

Direct-Seeded Rice (DSR) Water Management:
DSR fields are not puddled/flooded continuously; instead, irrigation is applied to maintain moist (not saturated) soil, with the first irrigation typically given 3-4 days after sowing and subsequent irrigations at 5-7 day intervals depending on rainfall, roughly halving total water use compared to transplanted flooded rice.

Rainfall Interaction:
Significant rainfall (20mm or more) can substitute for a scheduled irrigation in both flooded and DSR systems, though standing water fields should be checked for proper drainage to avoid prolonged submergence beyond 5-7 cm, which can damage young seedlings.

Signs of Water Stress in Rice:
Leaf rolling, drooping and a grayish tinge to leaves are visible signs of moisture stress that indicate irrigation is overdue, particularly critical to catch during flowering.
