WHEAT FERTILIZER RECOMMENDATIONS

General NPK Recommendation (irrigated, high-yielding varieties):
For irrigated wheat under normal soil fertility, a general recommendation is 120:60:40 kg/hectare of Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P2O5) and Potassium (K2O) respectively. Actual doses should be adjusted based on soil test results, since Punjab/Haryana soils are often already high in available Potassium, in which case K application can be reduced or skipped.

Application Timing (Split Application):
Nitrogen should be applied in split doses to improve use efficiency and reduce losses:
- 1/2 dose of Nitrogen + full dose of Phosphorus and Potassium at the time of sowing (basal application)
- 1/4 dose of Nitrogen at first irrigation (Crown Root Initiation stage, ~21 days after sowing)
- 1/4 dose of Nitrogen at second irrigation (tillering/jointing stage, ~45-50 days after sowing)

Applying all Nitrogen at sowing leads to excessive early vegetative growth, increased lodging risk, and nitrogen loss through leaching/volatilization before the crop can use it during later critical growth stages.

Micronutrients:
Zinc deficiency is common in wheat grown in alkaline or heavily rice-wheat rotated soils; a basal application of 25 kg/hectare zinc sulphate is recommended once every 2-3 years if soil tests indicate deficiency. Symptoms of zinc deficiency include interveinal chlorosis and stunted growth in patches.

Fertilizer Sources:
Common sources used are Urea (46% N), Diammonium Phosphate/DAP (18% N, 46% P2O5), Muriate of Potash/MOP (60% K2O), and Single Super Phosphate/SSP (16% P2O5, also supplies sulphur which benefits oilseed rotations).

Organic Supplementation:
Where available, farmyard manure (FYM) at 10-15 tonnes/hectare applied before sowing improves soil structure and nutrient availability, allowing a modest reduction (10-15%) in recommended chemical Nitrogen dose without yield penalty.

Stage-Specific Guidance for Farmer Queries:
If a farmer asks about fertilizer at the CRI/tillering stage specifically, the recommendation is a top-dressing of Urea (approximately 65-70 kg/hectare, supplying the 1/4 N split dose) applied just before or with the irrigation water for better uptake.
